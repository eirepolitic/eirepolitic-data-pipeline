## Slide 2 — Development (Strategic Gas Reserve) Bill 2026

**Status:** Enacted  
**Introduced by:** Minister for Climate, Energy and the Environment

### What it does

Creates a project-specific approval process for a State-led strategic gas emergency reserve at Cahiracon, Co. Clare. The reserve is intended as emergency backup if Ireland's normal gas supply is seriously disrupted.

### Main debate

**Case made for it:** Ireland imports most of its gas and currently has no emergency gas storage, so the Government argues a reserve is needed for security of supply during the transition to renewable energy.

**Concerns raised:** Deputies questioned the use of a bespoke planning route and sought stronger guarantees around emergency-only use, public control, climate safeguards and costs.

**EirePolitic record:** 67 certified Bill-linked transcript interventions • 3 certified linked divisions.

**Editorial note:** Do not turn the latest 90–57 linked division into a generic "support/oppose the Bill" graphic until the exact proposition is certified.

## Slide 3 — Israeli Settlements in the Occupied Palestinian Territory (Prohibition of Importation of Goods) Bill 2026

**Status:** Enacted  
**Introduced by:** Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade

### What it does

Prohibits importing goods into Ireland that originate in Israeli settlements in the occupied Palestinian territory. Importing those goods becomes an offence under the Customs Act 2015.

### Main debate

**Case made for it:** The Government framed the measure as part of Ireland's response to international-law obligations identified by the International Court of Justice.

**Concerns / scope debate:** A major question was whether the legislation should also cover services. The enacted Bill concerns goods; the Government said extending the measure to services raised additional legal difficulties.

**EirePolitic record:** 97 certified Bill-linked transcript interventions • 1 certified linked division.

**Editorial note:** The linked 67–79 division was on an amendment, not a final yes/no vote on the Bill. Never present it as the Bill's overall supporters and detractors.

Sources:
- Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade: https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-foreign-affairs/press-releases/minister-mcentee-welcomes-government-approval-of-text-of-the-israeli-settlements-prohibition-of-importation-of-goods-bill-2026/
- Department legislation page: https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-foreign-affairs/publications/israeli-settlements-in-the-occupied-palestinian-territory-prohibition-of-importation-of-goods-bill-2026/

## Slide 4 — Criminal Law, Civil Law and Defence (Miscellaneous Provisions) Bill 2026

**Status:** Enacted  
**Introduced by:** Minister for Justice, Home Affairs and Migration

### What it does

A wide-ranging Bill making changes across criminal law, courts, civil law and Defence. Its provisions include reforms to community sanctions, criminal evidence, court administration, international cooperation and Defence Forces powers.

### Main debate

**Case made for it:** The Minister argued the Bill allowed a large number of needed legal changes to be progressed together and addressed practical gaps across several areas of law.

**Concerns raised:** Senators and Deputies questioned the breadth and speed of the Bill, particularly the addition of significant Defence provisions and whether all parts received enough specialist scrutiny.

**EirePolitic record:** 192 certified Bill-linked transcript interventions • 3 certified linked divisions.

**Editorial note:** Keep the one-card summary broad; this Bill covers many unrelated provisions and is an obvious candidate for a later dedicated deep-dive post.

Source:
- Official Seanad Second Stage transcript: https://data.oireachtas.ie/ie/oireachtas/debateRecord/seanad/2026-06-18/debate/mul%40/main.pdf

## Slide 5 — Housing and Residential Tenancies (Miscellaneous Provisions) Bill 2026

**Status:** Enacted  
**Introduced by:** Minister for Housing, Local Government and Heritage

### What it does

Puts residency requirements for social-housing eligibility into legislation, creates a statutory appeals process for eligibility decisions, and makes further changes to residential-tenancy law.

### Main debate

**Case made for it:** The Government said the changes would create clearer, more consistent rules for social-housing eligibility across local authorities.

**Concerns raised:** Opposition members highlighted the complexity of how housing, immigration and EU law interact, and called for more scrutiny of significant amendments affecting housing and homelessness rules.

**EirePolitic record:** 108 certified Bill-linked transcript interventions • 3 certified linked divisions.

Sources:

## Slide 6 — Health (Provision of Contraception Prescribing Service in Retail Pharmacy Businesses) Bill 2026

**Status:** Enacted  
**Introduced by:** Minister for Health

### What it does

Provides the primary legal basis for a new community-pharmacy contraception service. The implementation being developed will allow pharmacists to provide repeat prescriptions for specified forms of contraception to women aged 17 and over following an initial GP prescription.

### Main implementation issue

The legislation is only one part of the rollout. Clinical protocols, professional guidance and pharmacist training are also required before the service operates in pharmacies.

**EirePolitic record:** 31 certified Bill-linked transcript interventions • no certified linked division in the current production data.

**Editorial note:** With no certified linked division, do not create a supporters/detractors graphic from debate speakers.

Sources:
- Pharmaceutical Society of Ireland, Contraception Prescribing Service: https://www.psi.ie/information-and-resources/contraception-prescribing-service
- Department of Health SME test: https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-health/publications/sme-test-enabling-pharmacist-prescribing-within-the-free-contraception-scheme-bill/

## Slide 7 — Regulation of Artificial Intelligence Bill 2026

**Status:** Enacted  
**Introduced by:** Minister for Enterprise, Tourism and Employment

### What it does

Builds Ireland's domestic enforcement system for the EU AI Act. It establishes an independent AI Office of Ireland as the central coordinating authority and gives regulators powers to supervise and enforce the EU rules.

### Main debate

**Case made for it:** Ireland needed the national regulatory structure required to make the EU AI Act work in practice, while giving businesses and regulators clearer rules.

**Concerns raised:** Deputies questioned the AI Office's independence and resourcing, how multiple regulators would coordinate, and whether protections around fundamental rights, children, privacy and work were strong enough.

**EirePolitic record:** 75 certified Bill-linked transcript interventions • 2 certified linked divisions.

Sources:
- Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment: https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/news-and-events/department-news/2026/june/20260617.html
- General Scheme: https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/legislation/general-scheme-of-the-regulation-of-artificial-intelligence-bill-2026.html

## Slide 8 — How to read this series

### A Bill can generate many debates and votes

EirePolitic links Bills to exact certified debate sections rather than assuming every speech or vote in the same sitting is about the Bill.

For this six-Bill test set, the pipeline finds **570 certified linked transcript interventions** and **12 certified linked divisions**.

A recorded division may be about an amendment rather than the Bill itself, so we only describe a vote as "support for" or "opposition to" a Bill when the exact proposition supports that interpretation.
