# Campaign brief — Planning for Constitutional Change Bill close vote

Status: **production package ready for design review; not published**

## Goal

Explain one of the closest recorded Dáil divisions in the current 2026 EirePolitic voting snapshot without misrepresenting it as a direct vote on Irish unity.

## Core fact

On **8 July 2026**, the Planning for Constitutional Change Bill 2026 was defeated at Second Stage:

- Tá: **69**
- Níl: **79**
- Staon: **0**
- Tá–Níl margin: **10 votes**

## Editorial thesis

The interesting story is not “69 TDs supported unity and 79 opposed it”. The Bill proposed a statutory planning process for possible constitutional change: a Government Green Paper, consultation, an all-island Citizens' Assembly and Oireachtas reporting.

Sinn Féin argued that preparation should begin before a referendum becomes imminent. Government opposition focused on the proposed mechanism and timetable, while continuing to frame constitutional change as a legitimate future possibility under the Good Friday Agreement.

## Audience takeaway

A close parliamentary vote can be politically important while still being narrower than the headline issue around it. Readers should leave understanding what the Bill actually proposed and why the 69–79 result cannot be treated as a poll of TDs on Irish unity.

## Format

1. Vote result / hook

## Visual direction

- neutral institutional/political visual language;
- primary visual is the **69 vs 79** vote split and **10-vote margin**;
- secondary visual is a simple process flow:
  `Green Paper → consultation → all-island Citizens' Assembly → Oireachtas reporting`;
- no pro-unity/anti-unity map treatment;
- no party-colour vote chart until event-date party attribution is certified.
