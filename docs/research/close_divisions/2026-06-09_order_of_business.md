# 9 June 2026 — Order of Business / migration debate request

## Formal vote

- **Type:** procedural Order of Business division.
- **Result:** Tá 78; Níl 69; Staon 0.
- **Margin:** 9.
- **Outcome:** the amendment was negatived and the proposed arrangements for the week's business were agreed.

## What was actually being voted on

The exact amendment was proposed by Matt Carthy under Standing Order 35(3). He sought to add **statements and questions on the EU Migration and Asylum Pact and the international protection system** to that week's Dáil schedule.

This matters because the same Order-of-Business exchange also contained requests about the Kenneally commission, special education and the Occupied Territories Bill. Those issues were part of the surrounding scheduling dispute but were **not** themselves the proposition in the 78–69 division.

## Debate context

Earlier that day Leaders' Questions had already featured a sharp Sinn Féin–Government exchange on the EU Migration and Asylum Pact. Mary Lou McDonald argued that opting into the pact weakened Irish control over migration policy and criticised delays in the international-protection system. Taoiseach Micheál Martin rejected the sovereignty claim and defended the Government's implementation approach.

During the Order of Business, Carthy argued that the level of public concern warranted a dedicated debate. Michael Collins separately backed an urgent migration-pact debate. The Government response was that the matter had already been debated extensively during the relevant legislation and declined the proposed scheduling change.

## Why the vote was close

### Confirmed

- The division was a vote on **whether to change that week's parliamentary schedule**, not a vote to repeal/approve the EU pact itself.
- Migration and international protection were politically contentious in the chamber that day.
- The division followed a direct Government/opposition dispute over whether another dedicated debate was needed.

### Supported interpretation

The narrow margin is best understood as a close **procedural coalition vote around opposition control of parliamentary time on a politically salient issue**, rather than evidence that the Dáil was within nine votes of reversing Ireland's participation in the pact.
